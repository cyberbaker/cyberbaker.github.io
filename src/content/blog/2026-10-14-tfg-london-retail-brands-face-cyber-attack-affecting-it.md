Customers of Phase Eight, Hobbs, and Whistles are facing uncertainty after their parent company, TFG London, identified a cyber-security incident affecting its IT systems. While physical stores remain open, some website functions are currently unavailable as the retailer investigates the breach.

This situation affects anyone who has recently shopped with these brands. When a retailer's systems are compromised, there is a risk that personal details could be accessed by unauthorised parties, which can lead to follow-on scams or identity theft.

## What happened

TFG London, the UK arm of the retail conglomerate The Foschini Group, identified an incident on Sunday that specifically impacted the Phase Eight, Hobbs, and Whistles brands. The company stated, "We are actively investigating and working with external cyber security specialists."

In a statement, TFG London apologised for the disruption and noted that it is working to identify if any sensitive customer information was impacted. The company told customers in an email that it did not believe it was necessary to change account login information at this time. That same email, seen by news service PA, also stated that the company did not believe customers' payment information had been affected. TFG London has said it will inform those whose personal information was impacted directly.

In a separate development, the fashion retailer H&M is investigating claims that it was targeted by a cyber-criminal group known as ShinyHunters. H&M stated it is aware of information circulating online regarding a potential incident but has not found indications that its data environment has been compromised.

These events follow a recent breach at Asos, where hackers used an app push notification—a message that pops up on a mobile phone screen—to act as a ransom note. In that case, hackers eventually obtained more detailed personal information belonging to Asos customers than the retailer initially suggested.

## Why it matters

These incidents represent more than just a temporary inconvenience on a website. If personal data is stolen, it can be used by criminals to launch targeted phishing attacks, which are fraudulent communications designed to trick people into revealing more sensitive information.

The recent activity suggests that major retail brands are frequently targeted by attackers. Beverley Griffiths, a senior lecturer in security and resilience at Bucks New University, noted that these attacks are a sign that large brands are firmly in the crosshairs of cyber criminals. 

For shoppers in the UK, the risk is that a breach at a favourite clothing retailer could lead to identity theft or unauthorised attempts to access financial accounts. As businesses hold increasing amounts of personal data to facilitate online shopping, they become high-value targets for those looking to exploit that information for financial gain.

## How this kind of attack works

The exact method used against TFG London has not been disclosed, but retail attacks often involve criminals searching for vulnerabilities in a company's IT systems to gain entry to databases. These databases often hold customer names, email addresses, and physical addresses.

Once inside, attackers may attempt to steal this data to sell on the dark web or use it for extortion. In some instances, they use ransomware (malicious software that locks an organisation's files and demands payment to unlock them) or simply threaten to release the stolen data publicly unless a payment is made.

In the case of the Asos breach, the attackers used an unusual method by hijacking the app's notification system to send messages directly to users' phones. This allowed them to communicate their demands directly to the victims, turning a standard service into a tool for intimidation. This method is considered an unprecedented move by cyber-security experts.

## What you can do

* **Monitor your bank statements.** Regularly checking your accounts for any unauthorised transactions can help you spot fraudulent activity early.
* **Be wary of unexpected communications.** If you receive an email or text message asking for personal details or password resets following a retail disruption, treat it with suspicion.
* **Report suspicious messages.** You can forward suspicious emails to report@phishing.gov.uk and suspicious text messages to 7726.
* **Use multi-factor authentication where available.** Enabling an extra layer of security on your retail and email accounts makes it harder for criminals to use stolen passwords.
* **Stay alert to direct updates.** Follow official channels from retailers like TFG London to confirm if your specific data has been compromised.

## The bigger picture

The frequency of these incidents has drawn comparisons to public transport, with Charlotte Wilson, head of enterprise at the cyber-security firm Check Point, suggesting that attacks on retailers are starting to arrive in clusters. It remains unclear if the current incidents are linked or if they represent a coordinated trend.

The retail industry faces questions about how exposed its systems have become to these types of attacks. Watch for direct updates from TFG London regarding whether any sensitive data was actually accessed.
