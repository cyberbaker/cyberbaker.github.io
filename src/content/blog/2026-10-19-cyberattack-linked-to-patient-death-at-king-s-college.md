A patient at King's College Hospital NHS Foundation Trust "died unexpectedly" following a cyberattack that disrupted vital medical services. A spokesperson for the trust stated that a "long wait for a blood test result" was one of several contributing factors in the death.

This incident is the first time a cyberattack has been directly linked to a death. The arrest of a suspect connected to the event brings the physical consequences of digital crime into focus.

## The arrest and the Synnovis hack

German police have detained a 28-year-old Russian man, identified as Vladimir K., who is accused of being a core member of the Qilin cybercrime gang. He was arrested by Japanese police in May while travelling abroad for a holiday, according to reports from The Verge.

The Qilin gang targeted Synnovis, a diagnostics and pathology service operated as a partnership between hospitals and the German company Synlab, on 3 June 2024. This attack caused severe disruption to blood transfusion services across several London hospitals. It led to the cancellation of operations, the diversion of emergency patients to other locations, and the disruption of more than 10,000 appointments.

The hackers threatened to publish private data on a dark net website—a hidden part of the internet used for anonymous activity—unless a ransom was paid. When no payment was made, the criminals published the highly private data online. While the hackers claimed the attack was a protest against the UK's lack of support in the war in Ukraine, their exact identity and motives have not been confirmed.

German officials are now including the Synnovis incident in their investigation of Vladimir K. Meanwhile, British police are examining whether their own inquiry may lead to an unlawful killing case.

## Why it matters

The Synnovis attack shows that cyberattacks can have physical, life-altering consequences. When a healthcare provider's data is inaccessible or stolen, the delay in accessing test results or managing blood supplies can directly impact patient safety.

For people in the UK, this highlights the vulnerability of the services we rely on daily. While much of the focus in cybersecurity is on protecting financial data or privacy, this incident shows that the integrity of clinical workflows is equally vital. It also shows the scale of international crime groups like Qilin, which has allegedly hacked more than 4,000 organisations and extorted more than $140m (£105m) since 2024.

## How this kind of attack works

The Qilin gang uses tactics to extort money from their victims. One method involves encrypting data, which means using software to scramble information so it cannot be read without a specific digital key.

In a typical attack, criminals gain access to an organisation's computer systems. Once inside, they can steal sensitive information and then deploy software to lock the organisation's data. To increase the pressure, they often use a tactic where they threaten to leak the stolen data on a dark net website if the victim refuses to pay.

In the case of Vladimir K., German officials allege he was involved in hacking a logistics company and encrypting its systems to extort approximately $160,000 in cryptocurrency (a digital form of currency used for online transactions). By disrupting the ability to read or use data, these groups can bring essential services, such as hospital pathology labs, to a standstill.

## What you can do

While individuals cannot prevent attacks on large hospital networks, you can take steps to protect your own digital presence and support better security practices.

* **Use strong, unique passwords** for every important account to prevent a single breach from affecting multiple services.
* **Enable multi-factor authentication** where possible, which requires a second form of identification to prove your identity.
* **Report suspicious communications** by forwarding phishing emails to report@phishing.gov.uk.
* **Keep software updated** on your personal devices to ensure you have the latest security defences against known vulnerabilities.

## The bigger picture

The arrest of Vladimir K. shows that international law enforcement is attempting to track and apprehend cybercriminals, even when they travel for leisure. However, because some jurisdictions, such as Russia, do not cooperate with Western law enforcement, catching these individuals remains difficult.

The investigation into the Synnovis hack will show how authorities approach the link between digital security and public safety. You may wish to monitor how your local NHS trust manages its data security and resilience.
